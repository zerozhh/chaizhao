# Fallacy Catalog — English Edition (62 primary entries + variant merges)

**Use**: For English-language texts, or as an English lookup mirror of `fallacies.md`. IDs (A1–H5) match the Chinese edition exactly, so eval cases and cross-references stay aligned. Output language follows the input language (see SKILL.md).

**Scope note**: Definitions, signals, and rebuttal lines are in English below. ## A. Concepts & Ambiguity

**A1 Equivocation** — *Def:* a key term silently shifts meaning between premise and conclusion. *Signal:* elastic words ("safe", "free", "fair") that blur under a definition request. *Rebut:* "Pin down what you mean by X first — then we'll argue."
*Variants:* persuasive definition; ambiguous middle (see H4).

**A2 Motte-and-Bailey** — *Def:* advance a bold claim; on pushback retreat to a modest one, then creep back. *Signal:* your rebuttals never land — you answer A, they reply A′, then re-assert A. *Rebut:* "Which version are you defending? We settle that one."

**A3 Weak Analogy** — *Def:* an analogy carries proof it can't support; the two things share only surface traits. *Signal:* "it's just like…" where the common point is shallow (both cost money, both feel bad). *Rebut:* "Analogies illustrate; they don't prove. These two differ exactly where it matters."

**A4 Composition / Division** — *Def:* what holds of parts is projected onto the whole (composition), or of the whole onto each part (division). *Signal:* "everyone did X, so you did X"; "the total is huge, so your share matters." *Rebut:* "What's true of the whole isn't thereby true of the parts."
*Variant:* ecological fallacy (group statistics applied to individuals).

**A5 Category Mistake** — *Def:* attributes of one category are welded onto another; an truth about A is used to settle a claim in B. *Signal:* "I don't need it, so your giving it isn't a gift"; "he's capable, so he needs no help." *Rebut:* "Those are two different categories — the first being true doesn't give you the second."

**A6 Amphiboly** — *Def:* structural (syntactic) ambiguity, not lexical: pronoun reference, dangling modifiers. *Signal:* "I saw the man on the hill with the telescope" — who has it? *Rebut:* "That sentence parses two ways — which one is your argument running on?"

**A7 Reification (Hypostatization)** — *Def:* an abstraction is treated as an agent with intents. *Signal:* "history will judge", "the market is afraid", "science says you must". *Rebut:* "History judges nobody — people do. Name the actual agent."

**A8 Incomplete Comparison** — *Def:* a comparative with no baseline: "3× faster" than what? *Signal:* superlatives and multiples with the referent missing. *Rebut:* "Three times faster than what, measured how?"
*Variant:* inconsistent comparison (different baselines per claim).

**A9 False Equivalence** — *Def:* two things alike in one respect are declared alike in the respect that matters. *Signal:* "they're all the same"; "everyone's corrupt, so no difference." *Rebut:* "'Both have problems' doesn't mean 'the problems are equal.'"
*Engine of:* whataboutism (see D2 variant).

**A10 Continuum Fallacy (Sorites)** — *Def:* because no precise boundary can be given, a difference is denied to exist. *Signal:* "what exact hour counts as staying up late? None? Then there's no such thing." *Rebut:* "A fuzzy boundary is still a boundary."

## B. Faulty Generalization

**B1 Hasty Generalization** — *Def:* a universal conclusion from a sample too small or unrepresentative. *Signal:* "young people nowadays all…" *Rebut:* "What you've seen ≠ everything there is."
*Variant:* stereotyping.

**B2 Anecdotal Fallacy** — *Def:* personal cases deployed against statistical evidence. *Signal:* "I know someone who smoked daily and lived to ninety." *Rebut:* "One exception doesn't beat a distribution — a lottery win doesn't make tickets an investment."
*Variant:* misleading vividness.

**B3 Survivorship Bias** — *Def:* only the "filtered survivors" are counted; the eliminated majority is invisible. *Signal:* every "all successful people have trait X" claim. *Rebut:* "You're only counting the planes that came back." (Wald's bomber analysis.)

**B4 Cherry Picking (Texas Sharpshooter)** — *Def:* conclusion first, data selected to fit; the arrow is drawn around the bullet hole. *Signal:* suspicious cutoffs, one-sided metrics, quietly swapped baselines. *Rebut:* "Where's the data you didn't pick?"
*Variants:* p-hacking; nut-picking (sampling only the opponents' extremists).

## C. Causation & Economics

**C1 False Cause** — *Def:* sequence or correlation treated as causation. *Signal:* "ever since X, Y"; "wherever X, also Y." *Rebut:* "Correlation isn't causation — what's the third variable?" (Ice cream sales and drowning rise together: summer.)

**C2 Broken Window Fallacy** — *Def:* the demand destruction "creates" is counted; the unseen displaced costs are not (Bastiat's candlemakers' petition, 1845). *Signal:* "X boosts GDP", "without X, who would pay for Y?" *Rebut:* "The key question: what's the unseen cost?"

**C3 Slippery Slope** — *Def:* A must inevitably slide to Z, though some link has no necessity. *Signal:* "today X, tomorrow Z." *Rebut:* "Why is the A→B step inevitable?" (Five steps at 80% each: under 33%.)

**C4 Reverse Causation** — *Def:* the causal arrow is flipped. *Signal:* one-way reading of bidirectional variables. *Rebut:* "Or Y causes X." (Cities with more firefighters have more fires.)

**C5 Single Cause Fallacy** — *Def:* a multi-factor outcome is pinned on one cause. *Signal:* "it's all because of X." *Rebut:* "It took several factors conspiring — name the others."
*Variant:* retrospective determinism ("looking back, it was inevitable").

## D. Person & Motive

**D1 Ad Hominem** — *Def:* attacking the speaker's character, identity, or situation instead of the claim. *Signal:* "would YOU believe someone like that?" *Boundary:* questioning *relevant* expertise is testimony assessment, not fallacy. *Rebut:* "Whether he's right has nothing to do with who he is."
*Variants:* Bulverism ("you only say that because you're X"); tone policing.

**D2 Tu Quoque** — *Def:* "you do it too" offered as refutation. *Signal:* "aren't you up past midnight too?" *Rebut:* "My failing to live it doesn't make it false."
*Variant:* whataboutism (deflecting via a third party's sins; often compounds A9/F5).

**D3 Appeal to Motive** — *Def:* "he's paid to say that" substitutes for engaging the evidence. *Signal:* "who's funding you?" *Rebut:* "Even if he's paid, the data is still the data."

**D4 Strawman** — *Def:* a distorted, easier-to-beat version of the opponent's position is attacked. *Signal:* replies to "so you're saying…" rather than to what was said. *Rebut:* "I didn't say that. Back to my actual words."
*Variants:* hollow man (attacking a position nobody held); nut-picking.

**D5 Contextomy (Quote Mining)** — *Def:* words quoted out of context to manufacture a meaning never intended. *Signal:* suspiciously short quotes, vague sourcing. *Rebut:* "Restore the two sentences around it and the meaning reverses."

**D6 Poisoning the Well** — *Def:* preemptive smear so everything the target later says is heard through it. *Signal:* "don't listen to him, he's an X." *Rebut:* "Let me finish — then decide what I am."

**D7 Genetic Fallacy** — *Def:* the claim is judged by its origin (inventor, era, etymology) rather than its evidence. *Signal:* "foreign idea, doesn't apply here"; "that word started as slang." *Rebut:* "Where an idea was born doesn't decide whether it's true."
*Variant:* etymological fallacy.

**D8 Association Fallacy (Guilt/Honor by Association)** — *Def:* a person's traits are inferred from the company or brands they keep. *Signal:* "he dines with X"; "you cited Y, so you're one of them." *Rebut:* "Who he associates with doesn't change whether the sentence is true."

## E. Appeals

**E1 False Authority** — *Def:* cross-field authorities, faded authorities, or a minority view dressed as consensus. *Boundary:* relevant-domain expert + real consensus + beyond-layperson topic = legitimate appeal. *Rebut:* "That expert's field isn't this."
*Variants:* appeal to accomplishment; courtier's reply ("you're not qualified to criticize").

**E2 Appeal to Popularity** — *Def:* many believers = true. *Signal:* "everyone says so"; "millions can't be wrong." *Rebut:* "Geocentrism was once unanimous."
*Variant:* regional/national version.

**E3 Appeal to Emotion** — *Def:* fear, guilt, pity, or outrage substituted for argument. *Signal:* "if you don't X, you have no heart." *Boundary:* emotionally relevant testimony is legitimate, merely insufficient; moral blackmail is the irrelevant version. *Rebut:* "Skip the label — show the math."
*Variants:* ad baculum (threat); appeal to pity; ridicule ("you, talk about logic?"); flattery; spite. *Mirror:* good-motive excuse ("my intentions were good, so no harm done").

**E4 Appeal to Tradition** — *Def:* longevity = correctness. *Signal:* "we've always done it this way." *Rebut:* "Tradition's age shows inertia, not merit." (Foot binding lasted a millennium.)

**E5 Appeal to Nature** — *Def:* natural = good, artificial = bad; a special case of E10. *Signal:* "all-natural, no additives." *Rebut:* "Arsenic is natural."
*Note:* the is–ought direction here runs natural→ought.

**E6 Appeal to Ignorance** — *Def:* "not disproven" treated as "proven." *Signal:* "you can't show I'm wrong, so I'm right." *Rebut:* "Russell's teapot: burden of proof rides with the claimant."
*Variants:* invincible ignorance; argument from silence.

**E7 Appeal to Consequences** — *Def:* a conclusion is rejected (or embraced) for being unpleasant (or pleasant). *Signal:* "imagine if that were true!"; "I refuse to believe it." *Rebut:* "Reality doesn't negotiate."
*Variant:* wishful thinking.

**E8 Just-World Fallacy** — *Def:* the world is assumed to run a fair reward system; outcomes retroactively assign virtue. *Signal:* "he's poor because he's lazy"; "flies don't bite clean skin." *Rebut:* "Outcomes are not character verdicts." (Wang Lung toils and stays landless.)

**E9 Appeal to Novelty** — *Def:* new = better, old = obsolete; mirror of E4. *Signal:* "latest generation, of course it's better." *Rebut:* "New and good are different axes."
*Variant:* chronological snobbery.

**E10 Is–Ought / Moralistic Fallacy** — *Def:* "it is so" → "it ought to be"; or "it ought to be" → "it is so." Both directions have no bridge. *Signal:* "he did it, so it must be acceptable"; "存在即合理" (whatever exists is reasonable); "good people don't get sick." *Rebut:* "'Is' and 'ought' don't commute." (Hume's guillotine.)

**E11 Appeal to Incredulity** — *Def:* "I can't imagine it" → false. *Signal:* "impossible!", "too absurd to be true." *Rebut:* "Your imagination hitting its limit isn't evidence about the world."
*Variant:* divine fallacy ("no explanation comes to mind, so: miracle/cheating").

## F. Debate Moves

**F1 False Dilemma** — *Def:* a spectrum compressed into two options. *Signal:* "either X or Y"; "not with us = against us." *Rebut:* "Between X and Y there's a whole plane."

**F2 Begging the Question (Circular)** — *Def:* the conclusion smuggled into the premises. *Signal:* "he's always right, because he never errs." *Rebut:* "That's the answer wearing an argument's clothes."
*Variants:* mutually supporting circle; loaded label.

**F3 Burden Shifting** — *Def:* the claimant demands disproof instead of providing proof. *Signal:* "prove it's harmless, or admit it's harmful." *Rebut:* "Burden of proof is yours." (Russell's teapot again.)
*Variant:* "I'm entitled to my opinion" (a right to speak ≠ exemption from evidencing).

**F4 Moving the Goalposts** — *Def:* each time the evidence lands, the standard is upgraded. *Signal:* evidence delivered, criteria quietly raised. *Rebut:* "You set that bar. Come back to it."
*Variant:* invincible ignorance as a universal exemption.

**F5 Red Herring** — *Def:* a new topic dragged across the trail so the original issue escapes. *Signal:* answering by pivoting; "that reminds me…" *Rebut:* "We'll get to that — answer the original question first."

**F6 Relative Privation ("Suffering Olympics")** — *Def:* a bigger suffering is used to cancel the legitimacy of a smaller concern. *Signal:* "people are starving and you complain about X?" *Rebut:* "Compassion isn't zero-sum — and by this logic no topic ever survives."

**F7 Nirvana Fallacy** — *Def:* a real option rejected because it isn't perfect. *Signal:* "it won't fix everything, so do nothing." *Rebut:* "The fair comparison is status quo vs. next-best — not status quo vs. utopia." ("Better late than never" cuts both ways.)

**F8 Argument by Assertion (Ipse Dixit)** — *Def:* repetition without argument; familiarity feigning truth. *Signal:* maximal volume, zero premises. *Rebut:* "Say it a hundred times — it's still an assertion."
*Variants:* ad lapidem (dismissing as "absurd" with no reasons); thought-terminating clichés ("it is what it is", "don't overthink it").

**F9 Argument to Moderation (False Compromise)** — *Def:* the midpoint between two positions is declared correct because it's the midpoint. *Signal:* "it takes two to tango"; "split the difference." *Rebut:* "The truth isn't found by averaging — one says the earth is flat, one says round; the answer isn't 'a bit bumpy.'"

**F10 Two Wrongs Make a Right** — *Def:* the opponent's wrongdoing used to license one's own. *Signal:* "he swung first"; "everyone does it." *Rebut:* "His wrong is on his ledger; yours is on yours."

**F11 No True Scotsman** — *Def:* on counterexample, the set is redefined to expel it — "no TRUE fan does that." *Signal:* definitions rewritten after the evidence arrives. *Rebut:* "Fix the definition first, then we'll examine examples."

**F12 Special Pleading (Ad Hoc Rescue)** — *Def:* exemptions granted for one's own claim that are denied to all rivals; unfalsifiable rescue conditions added on the fly. *Signal:* "my case is special"; "this time is different because…" *Rebut:* "May others use that exemption too?"

**F13 Loaded Question (Many Questions)** — *Def:* a question that smuggles in an un conceded premise — any answer concedes it. *Signal:* "when did you stop cheating?" *Rebut:* "I reject the premise of that question."

## G. Statistics & Probability

**G1 Base Rate Neglect** — *Def:* prior probabilities ignored; small conditional numbers carry the argument. *Signal:* "the test is 99% accurate, positive means you have it." *Rebut:* "Start from the base rate: 1/1000 disease, 99% sensitivity — most positives are healthy."
*Variant:* prosecutor's fallacy (P(evidence|innocent) vs P(innocent|evidence)).

**G2 Gambler's Fallacy** — *Def:* independent events assigned memory: "it's due." *Signal:* "ten reds in a row — black next." *Rebut:* "The coin doesn't remember."

**G3 Regression Fallacy** — *Def:* natural mean reversion credited to an intervention that happened to coincide. *Signal:* "scolded him and he improved"; "prayed and the fever broke"; the Sports Illustrated cover "curse." *Rebut:* "Extreme performances drift back on their own."

**G4 McNamara Fallacy** — *Def:* only the quantifiable is real; what can't be measured doesn't count. *Signal:* "data or it didn't happen." *Rebut:* "Unmeasured ≠ nonexistent." (Cousin: Goodhart's law — when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure.)

**G5 Sunk Cost Fallacy** — *Def:* unrecoverable past investment used to justify further investment. *Signal:* "we've queued two hours; leaving now would waste them." *Rebut:* "The past hours are gone either way — only the next one matters."

**G6 Conjunction Fallacy** — *Def:* a more specific (conjunctive) story judged MORE likely than a broader event — P(A∧B) ≤ P(A), always. *Signal:* "she reads poetry and studies literature — more likely a librarian than a farmer." *Rebut:* "Detail is a tax on probability." (Tversky & Kahneman's Linda problem.)

## H. Formal Fallacies (structure itself invalid)

For formalization steps and the valid/invalid form table, see `formal-checks.md`.

**H1 Affirming the Consequent** — *Form:* If P→Q; Q; ∴P. ✗ *Signal:* "If it rains, the ground is wet. The ground is wet — so it rained." (Someone could have hosed it.) *Rebut:* "The premise explains the result; the result doesn't prove the premise."

**H2 Denying the Antecedent** — *Form:* If P→Q; ¬P; ∴¬Q. ✗ *Signal:* "Hard work gets you grades; he didn't work hard; so he'll fail." (Other paths exist.) *Rebut:* "One road being closed doesn't close them all."

**H3 Undistributed Middle** — *Form:* All A are C; all B are C; ∴ all A are B. ✗ *Signal:* "Doctors wear white coats; chefs wear white coats; so doctors are chefs." *Rebut:* "Sharing one umbrella category makes nobody identical."

**H4 Fallacy of Four Terms** — *Def:* a fourth concept sneaks into a syllogism — the middle term means two things. *Signal:* "rat poison is a drug; drugs are good; so rat poison is good." *Rebut:* "That middle word isn't the same word twice." (Grounds: A1.)

**H5 Appeal to Probability** — *Form:* possible ⇒ treated as actual/certain. *Signal:* "what if though?", "anything's possible, so you can't rule it out." *Rebut:* "'Possible' starts a probability estimate; it doesn't end an argument."

**Long tail** (rare; file under these names when detected): affirming a disjunct, existential fallacy, illicit major/minor, exclusive premises, modal fallacies — see `formal-checks.md`.

## English-Discourse Native Variants (归并索引)

Discourse patterns especially common in English arguments, mapped to primary entries:

- **Sealioning** — perpetual "polite" demands for evidence to exhaust the opponent → F3 (burden shifting) weaponized
- **Gish gallop** — flooding with dozens of weak claims faster than they can be answered → rebuttal strategy: pick the load-bearing one, name the flood (F5-family move at debate scale)
- **JAQing off** — "Just ASKING questions" to insinuate while disclaiming → F13 (loaded question) in serial form
- **Concern trolling** — feigned solicitude used to undermine ("I totally agree, but aren't you worried people will think…") → D3/E3 hybrid
- **What aboutism** — see D2 variant
- **Nut-picking** — see B4/D4 variants
- **Motte-and-bailey** — A2
- **Kettle logic** — mutually contradictory defenses offered simultaneously → structurally F2-family; flag as inconsistent defense set

These are *labels for recognition*; the analysis output should still use the primary entry ID + variant in parentheses (e.g., "F3（sealioning）").
